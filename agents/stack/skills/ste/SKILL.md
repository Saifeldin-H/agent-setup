# Simplified Technical English

Use these rules for reports and plans addressed to the user. Preserve facts, evidence, and the author's meaning when editing.

## Write sentences the reader can act on

- State one thought per sentence. Aim for about 20 words in an instruction and 25 in other sentences.
- Write instructions as direct commands. Put conditions and warnings before the action they govern.
- Use active voice and name the actor when it matters. Address the reader as "you" when giving directions.
- Keep articles and verbs. Use complete sentences. Spell out arrows and unfamiliar abbreviations.
- Give each thing one name and each word one meaning. Use exact file, symbol, and command names. Keep modifiers next to what they qualify and give pronouns clear referents.
- Prefer everyday words and literal descriptions. Use "is", "has", and "use". Replace jargon, idioms, metaphors, and weak verbs with concrete terms.

## Edit for facts and readability

- Name the source or remove the attribution. Give the mechanism, number, or observed result. Keep one hedge when the evidence needs it.
- Cut empty openers, canned conclusions, filler, empty trailing phrases, and adverbs that add no fact. State the main point in a direct sentence.
- Include each item readers need. Let the content determine how many items to use. Give ordered steps numbers and use bullets for unordered items. Use a range only when its endpoints share a scale.
- Use sentence case for headings and straight quotes. Use bold only when it helps a reader find something. Skip decorative emoji and labels that restate their lines.
- Use periods and commas. When an aside matters, make it a sentence. Introduce lists and examples with a sentence.
